The first 90 days of a fractional growth engagement.

In the first 90 days of a fractional growth engagement, three things should happen in order. Days 1 to 30: diagnose, and make the measurement trustworthy. Days 31 to 60: cut waste and rebuild the account and key pages on that foundation. Days 61 to 90: start structured testing, hand over a weekly number, and decide together what comes next. The order matters more than the dates. Optimising before the numbers are true is the most common way these engagements waste their first quarter.

This is the sequence I run. It's also a reasonable yardstick for anyone you hire in the role, whether that's me or someone else.

Before day one: access and a baseline

Two things I ask for before starting. First, admin access to everything: ad accounts, analytics, tag manager, the website, the CRM. If any of those sit in a previous agency's ownership, getting them back becomes job one. Second, one number from outside the ad platforms. Revenue, signed customers, booked jobs, whatever the business counts when it counts honestly. Everything gets measured against that.

Days 1 to 30: diagnose and make the numbers true

Week 1 to 2: audit. I go through the ad accounts, the tracking and the funnel and write down what I find, ranked by how much money each problem is costing. The four areas are the same ones in the growth engine audit: tracking and data, paid acquisition, website and conversion, systems and process. You get a written fix-list, not a slide deck.

Week 2 to 4: fix measurement. In almost every account I take on, something in the conversion tracking is wrong: forms that count on click instead of on success, the same conversion counted twice, phone calls not counted at all, or a "lead" that includes spam and job applicants. I repair it, then check platform numbers against the outside number from day zero. Until those agree within a tolerance we've both accepted, nothing else gets optimised. The twelve signs are what I'm checking for.

Also in month one: stop the obvious bleeding. Some waste doesn't need better data to identify. Search terms that are plainly irrelevant, ads running in countries you don't serve, display placements nobody chose. Those get cut in the first fortnight. This note covers where it usually hides.

What you should have at day 30: the written audit, tracking you can trust, the first savings, and a plan for month two that you've agreed to.

Days 31 to 60: rebuild on the new foundation

Restructure the account. With conversions now meaning something, campaigns get rebuilt around how the business sells: by service line, by market, by margin. Bidding moves from clicks or raw leads to qualified outcomes. Where the sale closes offline, I start feeding those outcomes back with offline conversion imports, so the platform learns which leads were worth having.

Fix the highest-leverage page. Usually one landing page or form carries most of the paid traffic. It gets the first round of conversion work: load time, a single clear call to action, a shorter form, a clear next step. For a chauffeur client this stage meant building an instant quote estimator, which changed both the conversion rate and the quality of the signal going back to Google Ads.

Set up the weekly number. One report, one page: spend, qualified outcomes, cost per qualified outcome, by channel. Someone on your side is named as its owner.

Expect a dip. When you change what counts as a conversion and restructure campaigns, automated bidding has to relearn. Reported conversions often fall, partly because the old number was inflated and partly because the algorithm is recalibrating. I say this at the start so it isn't a surprise in week six. It typically settles within a few weeks.

What you should have at day 60: a restructured account bidding on qualified outcomes, one improved page live, and a weekly report you believe.

Days 61 to 90: start compounding

Structured tests. Now that the numbers are reliable, tests mean something. One hypothesis at a time: an offer, a page layout, a bidding target, a new campaign type. Each gets written up, win or lose.

Documentation and handover. Everything built so far gets documented: what fires where, why campaigns are structured the way they are, how to read the report. The test of good documentation is whether your team could keep the system running for a month if I disappeared.

The day-90 review. We compare against the baseline using your number, not the platform's. Then one of three things happens. You take it in-house, with the documentation. We continue on a lighter cadence. Or the work has grown into a full-time job, and I help you hire for it. All three are good outcomes.

Month one makes the numbers true. Month two acts on them. Month three is where it starts to compound.

Signs it's off track

Use these on me or anyone else.

At day 30: no written findings. Tracking still unverified. You've had meetings but nothing has changed in any account.

At day 60: reporting still quotes platform conversions with no reconciliation to revenue. Work is being done in accounts you don't own. You can't name what shipped.

At day 90: no documentation. No clear answer to "what would we lose if you left?" beyond "the operator".
